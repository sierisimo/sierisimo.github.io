## A Year Has Passed Since My Last Post

It has been a year since I set up a GitHub pages associated to my domain just to try stuff, just to keep things out of
my mind… maybe more than a year. I tried different static site generators because I always think:

"I will just do something simple, something that I setup once and I don't have to worry again for it for
a few months/years..." and yet, almost a year has passed since I setup [Zola][zola] becuase it was "simpler"
than [Hugo][hugo] and more "robust" than [Marmite][marmite] and then,
the usual happened, I started fantasizing with things I could do, how I could move my templates to Elm or handlebars,
then integrate styles in SCSS as I used to 15 years ago (back then it was SASS), how I will write about everything
and maybe add videos... many ideas, almost no real output/implementations.

And I'm tired of cheating on myself, honestly. I feel like "Professor Calamitous" from Jimmy Neutron: an evil scientist
that always leaves his projects half baked. He misses a bathroom in his evil robot, he misses finishing a filled donut,
etc. I feel similar, I never completed most of my side projects and I'm always hearing other people talking about the
updates on their apps they published, the new LLM model they are trying, the new videos on their channel, their new hobbies.

A year ago, I was doing great, I was starting Capoeira, I was living on my own house and having a blast in my previous
job. But still, the laziness of every day stopped me to actually complete things… going to the third floor to turn on
the computer just to write a blog about how I dislike LLMs? Naaa, maybe tomorrow. Solving one more of those pending
exercises I was doing? Exercises will be there tomorrow… you get the drill.

It's not that I'm depressed or anything, I was actually enjoying things, it was at some point too much around to
actually focus on things I wanted or fantasized to do.

**But here I am again.**

In the past I searched for services to translate my posts or asked for reviews to a few friends
(_Hey Nicole! Hey Jhoon! Ana!!!_) but I think that only lead to more issues, and now that LLMs are scrapping
everything, I think is worth having a few typos, to be less correct and just spread words.

Another huge problem I frequently find is _the side quest hero_…

- Hey, let's add a linter for my Markdown…
- What about a dictionary? I like to look like I know grammar…
- What if I just configure the right LSP for this…
- Maybe I can configure everything in another format…
- Let's rebuild the whole template with another theme…
- Wait wait wait… If I write a small Gradle plugin I may publish on [Medium][medium], [Dev.to][devto]
and my repo in one hit
- …but if it is a GitHub action it will be automatically on push…
- …and maybe, it's better if it's a Rust library…
- …so I should practice rust and start a new crate…

You get the idea… but **I REALLY HOPE THIS TIME**, is more about writing and the technical stuff I like
than overcomplicated setups for just keeping a bunch of Markdown.

"_But why don't you delegate those to an LLM?_", welp, where's the fun on that? Setting up a bunch
of clankers on my machine just to try to organize and make my posts look cool and automated?

Naaa, I'm okay with being a caveman that uses his hands.

## On What's Next

In the age of LLMs, is it worth blogging "by hand"? I think so.

I will try to come back more frequently, if this thing works out as way to express myself,
I hope it becomes a habit, as writing is the only known way for now that I have to be rebellious.
